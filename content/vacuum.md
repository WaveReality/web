The **vacuum** is the modern term for the properties of "empty" space, replacing the classical notion of the [[aether]]. Like the aether, "empty" space is anything but empty according to the [[Standard Model]]. Instead, it is filled by the [[Higgs]] field and the _quantum foam_ of [[virtual particles]] that can pop in and out of existence at random. These properties of the vacuum give rise to physically measurable effects, and must therefore be considered "real".

Furthermore, the quantum [[field theory]] upon which the Standard Model is based requires 236 unique real-valued numbers to represent the unique degrees of freedom in the relevant fields, as computed in [[Standard Model#Components]]. These fields are thought to pervade empty space as well, and are logically necessary to enable the virtual particles to emerge anywhere.

The existence of all these quantum fields, especially the [[Higgs]] field which is specifically required to have a non-zero value at all points in space due to spontaneous symmetry breaking, creates one of the largest discrepancies between the predictions of the Standard Model and actual data (see this [Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_constant_problem)), one that [[^@HobsonEfstathiouLasenby06]] described as "probably the worst theoretical prediction in the history of physics".

See [[zero-point]] for further discussion and frameworks that attempt to make use of the zero point vacuum state to explain various phenomena.
