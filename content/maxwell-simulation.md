<div>

This simulation runs the [[Maxwell]] wave equations. There are multiple sections exploring different electromagnetic (EM) wave phenomena. This should be helpful in better understanding the relationship between the four-potential $A_\mu$ and the electrical vector field `E` which is the gradient of the potential, and `B` which is the curl of it.

## Electrical potential

The electrical potential $A_0$ is represented by the value `A0s` in the simulation. The default `Electric Potential` [[#sim_em:Config]] sets up a fixed point charge at the center of the state space (see the `Source` in `Config` entries at the left-hand side for the location: -1 means the center).

As you [[#sim_em:Step 1]] this simulation, you can see the `A0s` value spread out to the $1/r$ curve that then corresponds to a $1/r^2$ curve in terms of the gradient of `A0s`, which is what drives the force on another charged particle.

The `Charge Dipole` Config is similar, but sets up two opposite charges, where you can see that the distant field state falls off as $1/r^3$ because the two opposing potentials largely cancel each other out, leaving a small residual.

The `A0NoWave` flag in `Params` controls whether the updating of the `A0s` value uses the _diffusion_ equation (`A0NoWave = true`) or the standard wave equation (`A0NoWave = false`). In the diffusion equation, the neighbor-based potential force generated by the Laplacian is applied directly to the velocity, instead of _adding_ to the existing velocity factor, like an acceleration term should. When this is on (as it is by default), it makes the `A0` potential propagation more robust, and more similar to the Coulomb gauge. 

## EM waves

The `Linear Polarization` config creates an EM wave with linear polarization in the _E_ and _B_ fields along the transverse dimensions. The three other panels show the `A`, `E` and `B` fields using the `Vector` display, which shows the X,Y,Z components as vector lines pointing in the direction of these vector fields. 

Press `Step 1` a few times and you'll see all the vector fields light up. You can use your mouse scroll functionality to zoom into the 3D display to get a better view of the vectors (also the bottom toolbar Zoom in button too). You should see that the `A` vector potential is pointing up and down in the Y direction, which causes a corresponding `E` gradient in the same Y axis, while the `B` field is in the Z axis due to the way that the curl works.

Thus, you can see the expected phenomenon where the `E` and `B` waves oscillate 90 degrees out of phase with each other, along the two different transverse axes (Y and Z) relative to the direction of wave travel (X).

The `Polarization` axis is selectable in the `Config` settings.

The `Circular Polarization` config creates a circularly polarized wave configuration, which just has a different phase relationship that emerges in the E and B fields.

## Charge current source

The `Moving Charge` configuration simulates a moving beam of electrical charge current, along the X axis, which allows you to see the resulting magnetic `B` waves created surrounding this current. Use the Up and Down arrow buttons on the lower view toolbar to move the Y plane slice up and down, to see how the B field curls around the moving charge.
