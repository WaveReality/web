A **lepton** is one of two categories of [[fermion]] particles, which are those  having [[spin]] 1/2 and a non-zero rest mass. The [[electron]] is the prototypical member of the lepton category, which also includes uncharged [[neutrino]]s, and 2 other [[generation]]s of electrons (_muon_ and _tau_).

The charged leptons have an electrical charge of -1, and each have an [[antimatter]] particle with the opposite charge.

{id="table_leptons" title="charged leptons"}
| Name     | Mass kg           | Mass MeV/$c^2$ | Mass ratio | Antiparticle |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|------------|--------------|
| electron | $9.1 x 10^{-31}$  | 0.51           | 1          | positron     |
| muon     | $1.88 x 10^{-28}$ | 105            | 207        | antimuon     |
| tau      | $3.17 x 10^{-27}$ | 1,776          | 3,484      | antitau      |

The higher generations are unstable and ultimately decay back into an electron along with various neutrinos.

There are also 3 corresponding generations of neutrinos, which have much smaller mass differences. The antineutrinos differ only in their spin direction.

Explaining the nature of this **mass hierarchy** across generations, and the relationship between charged and uncharged leptons, is a major goal of the [[spinfield]] model.
