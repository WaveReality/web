The phenomenon of **zitterbewegung**, which means "bouncing back and forth" in German, was originally proposed by [[@^Schrodinger30]], as a very high-frequency vibration at twice the Compton frequency (see [[planck relation]]) ([[@Hestenes90]]). It is largely beyond current experimental techniques to measure (e.g., frequency of around $2.5 \times 10^{20}$ Hz), but some indications have been observed ([[@GerritsmaKirchmairZahringerEtAl10]]; [[@QuHamnerGongEtAl13]]).

The [[Weyl]] equations for an [[electron]] exhibit strong zitterbewegung oscillations in terms of a coupling between two first-order helical waves, while the second-order [[Dirac]] description ends up being four fully-independent wave equations, which only [[spin]] in the presence of an EM field. Thus, it is possible that empirical detection of zitterbewegung could discriminate between these two descriptions of the electron.

Furthermore, the de Broglie version of the [[pilot wave]] framework, where the particle serves as a source input driving the wave oscillation, predicts such a phenomenon as well ([[@DaganBush20]]).
