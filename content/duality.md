There are two seemingly-contradictory entities at the heart of the quantum realm: [[quantum wave]]s and [[particle]]s, i.e., the fundamental wave-particle **duality**. Many aspects of the confusion and paradox in quantum physics is tied up with this duality. For example, the standard [[Copenhagen]] interpretation adopts an [[epistemic]] approach to the quantum wave (i.e., the wave reflects the state of our knowledge, and is not actually a physically real thing), which makes it difficult to understand what is happening in the [[double-slit]] experiment: how does an epistemic wave cause _real_ interference effects?

The [[pilot-wave]] framework instead embraces the simultaneous and interacting _reality_ of both particles and waves, and thus provides a simple explanation for the double-slit experiment: the particle goes through one slit, while the wave flows through _both_ slits, and gives rise to the observed interference effects.

See [[mechanisms]] for a more detailed discussion for why the pilot-wave ontology seems to be essential for providing a (mostly) sensible understanding of the quantum realm.
