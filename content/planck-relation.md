The **Planck relation** is one of the most important, simple equations in all of quantum physics, specifying the relationship between **energy** _E_ and wave **frequency** _f_, in terms of **Planck's constant** _h_:

{id="eq_planck" title="Planck relation"}
$$
E = h f
$$

The relationship between wavelength and frequency also contains a speed-of-light factor:

{id="eq_lambda-c" title="frequency-wavelength relationship"}
$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{f}
$$

So the wavelength as a function of energy is:

{id="eq_lambda" title="Planck wavelength for energy"}
$$
\lambda = \frac{h c}{E}
$$

For a particle at rest, the energy is a function of its rest mass $m_0$:

{id="eq_emc" title="Einstein's mass-energy relation"}
$$
E_0 = m_0 c^2
$$

Which determines a corresponding frequency:

{id="eq_freq" title="rest frequency"}
$$
f_0 = \frac{m_0 c^2}{h}
$$

And a wavelength, known as the **Compton wavelength**:

{id="eq_lambda-0" title="rest (Compton) wavelength"}
$$
\lambda_0 = \frac{h}{m_0 c}
$$

In the [[pilot-wave]] framework, the [[quantum wave]] vibrates at this wavelength. The approximate length scales for different particles are shown in [[#table_compton]]. The proton is a composite of 3 quarks, with an estimated charge radius of $8.40 x 10^{-16}$, which is smaller than its Compton wavelength by mass. 

{id="table_compton" title="Compton wavelengths"}
| Particle | rest mass (MeV/c^2) | wavelength (m)    |
|----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| electron | 0.51                | $2.43 x 10^{-12}$ |
| muon     | 105                 | $1.17 x 10^{-14}$ |
| tau      | 1,177               | $6.98 x 10^{-16}$ |
| proton   | 938                 | $1.32 x 10^{-15}$ |
