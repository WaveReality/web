In the [[Standard Model]], various wave equations including the [[Dirac]] and [[Weyl]] equations are used to represent the wave aspect of [[fermion]] particles like the [[electron]]. In the standard [[Copenhagen]] and other [[interpretations]], this wave is thought to be purely [[epistemic]] in nature, reflecting a lack of knowledge about the underlying quantum state until a measurement takes place.

In the [[pilot-wave]] model, the **quantum wave** is instead considered to be a physically real (**ontological**) entity, that interacts with massive [[particle]]s, thus creating the unambiguously real interference effects and other such phenomena that are at the heart of quantum physics (e.g., the [[double-slit]] experiment results).

Even within the pilot-wave model, these wave equations represent both epistemic and ontologically real aspects of the time-evolution of a particle. An important goal of the [[Spinfield Model]] is to "unscramble this omelette" and develop a quantum wave function that only reflects the ontologically real impact of quantum waves, with the time-evolution of particles under [[stochastic motion]] generating the epistemic aspects.

Given that nobody has yet unscrambled this omelette, determining exactly what belongs in this quantum wave is a difficult process. In principle, one could develop a more solid, well-grounded model of stochastic motion and other properties such as [[spin]] that properly belong to the individual particles, and then effectively subtract this from the [[Dirac]] wave function, with the remainder representing what the quantum wave needs to capture. See [[mechanisms]] for more detailed discussion.
