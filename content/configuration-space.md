**Configuration space** is a critical but perhaps generally underappreciated element of standard quantum mechanics, in most of its various formulations (e.g., in the [[Hilbert space]] formulation). It is the space defined by the **multiparticle** configuration of all the elements of relevance to a given experimental setup being analyzed.

Because it describes the _configuration_ of these elements, it is **exponential** in size, with a different space corresponding to each combination of such elements, and manifestly [[non-locality|non-local]]. Thus, it is an entirely implausible, highly problematic element of standard quantum mechanical approaches, including the existing [[pilot-wave]] models.

The need for configuration space at a mathematically deep level arises because the equations being used are _linear_, so they cannot represent any kind of actual interaction among different particles. Without configuration space, every particle would fully superpose on every other particle --- they would just slip on past each other. This is in fact how _bosons_ (e.g., [[photon]]s) behave, but not how _fermions_ like [[electron]]s behave.

The [[pilot-wave]] approach has been criticized for using configuration space, because it posits that the wave function is actually a "real" thing, thus exposing the implausibility of this otherwise purely [[tools vs models|calculational tool]].

[[@^NorsenMarianOriols15]] analyzed the contributions of configuration space to the pilot-wave results. They concluded that indeed the configuration space contains a large amount of "redundant" information, and that even the simplest approximation for the inter-particle interaction terms does a reasonable (yet imperfect) job of capturing the behavior of the full configuration-space model. Exploration of higher-order terms in this approximation are ongoing ([[@Norsen22]]).

The single most important implication of the distinction between calculational tools versus models is the recognition that **configuration space is a calculational tool that allows linear math to describe nonlinear physical processes**. While this tool is very handy for doing certain high-level calculations, the exponentially explosive character of the resulting formalism should be a strong indication that it is not how the underlying physics works.

Indeed, the use of Feynman diagrams in the [[Standard Model]], which imply discrete, localized, nonlinear interactions that are contingent on energy thresholds and various other forms of conservation rules, clearly indicates exactly where these nonlinearities arise. The abstract frameworks of Hilbert space and the linear [[Schodinger]] wave function are clearly not directly capturing these essential nonlinearities present within the actual detailed formalism that is necessary for describing how the underlying physics actually operates. This discrepancy is somehow not widely recognized in the [[interpretations]] of the physical reality implied by quantum physics.
